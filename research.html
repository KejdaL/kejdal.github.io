Research
  • Building Futures: The Impact of Affordable Housing on Student Achievement (Job Market Paper: Paper )
    • This paper examines how nearby Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) development affects student engagement and academic achievement. I link geocoded HUD LIHTC data with longitudinal administrative records for approximately 2.8 million student-year observations from Wisconsin public schools and use a spatial, staggered difference-in-differences design. LIHTC exposure reduces attendance by 0.69 percentage points and increases chronic absenteeism by 2.28 percentage points. Effects accumulate over time, while achievement effects are more limited: math scores increase by 0.042 standard deviations among middle-school students and 0.021 standard deviations among high-school students, with little change in ELA scores. The findings show that place-based housing investments can generate meaningful spillovers for nearby students and schools.
  • Affordable Housing, School Composition, and Institutional Adjustment: Evidence from LIHTC Placement (Working Paper)
    • This paper examines whether affordable housing development reshapes nearby public schools as institutions. Using a Wisconsin school-year panel and a spatial, staggered difference-in-differences design, I estimate the effects of nearby LIHTC placement on racial and ethnic composition, economically disadvantaged enrollment, school diversity, and accountability outcomes. LIHTC placement increases racial and ethnic diversity and the share of economically disadvantaged students while reducing overall school accountability scores. The findings highlight an important institutional dimension of place-based housing policy: affordable housing development can change not only where households live, but also the composition and demands faced by nearby public schools.
  • Housing Market Shocks, Affordability, and Cognitive Aging: Evidence from the Great Recession (Working Paper)
    • This project examines whether housing-market shocks during the Great Recession affected subsequent cognitive aging among older adults. I link restricted longitudinal Health and Retirement Study data with county-level measures of house prices, rents, unemployment, income, and housing affordability. The analysis examines several mechanisms through which housing-market disruptions may influence later-life health, including housing-wealth losses, mortgage distress, and housing-cost burden, with particular attention to households facing greater financial vulnerability.
  • Breaking Barriers to Access: Medicaid Coverage, Take-Up, and Exit of GLP-1s (Working Paper)
    • This study examines how state Medicaid coverage policies affect utilization of GLP-1 medications for obesity. I construct a state-by-quarter panel using Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services State Drug Utilization Data from 2019 through 2025 and exploit variation in the timing of state coverage adoption. Medicaid coverage is associated with an increase of approximately 11.5 prescriptions for obesity-specific GLP-1 medications per 1,000 enrollees per quarter. The analysis also examines how effects evolve over time, differences between fee-for-service and managed-care utilization, and changes in state coverage policy, including subsequent exits.
  • Hospital Market Structure and Medical Debt Collections (Working Paper)
    • This project examines the relationship between hospital market structure and household medical debt. We link hospital-market information from the American Hospital Association with consumer-credit data from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York/Equifax Consumer Credit Panel. The analysis documents geographic variation in medical collections across counties and hospital referral regions and examines how differences and changes in hospital market structure relate to household financial exposure.
  • Nowcasting Food Hardship: Applying Machine Learning to Aggregate Retail Sales Data (Under Review)
    • This study develops tools to nowcast food hardship using national and state-level Household Pulse Survey data and USDA Weekly Retail Food Sales data. We use Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) methods to identify retail-sales measures that predict food insufficiency. Food spending, unit sales, and spending across specific food categories provide predictive information, demonstrating the potential for high-frequency retail data to complement traditional measures of household food hardship.
